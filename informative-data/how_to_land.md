The Orion spacecraft, launched on NASA’s Space Launch System (SLS) rocket, is used for crewed missions around the Moon, while actual surface landings will rely on a separate Human Landing System (HLS) (such as those developed by SpaceX or Blue Origin) that astronauts transfer to from Orion. [1] (https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/artemis/), [2] (https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-iii/), [3] (https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-iv/)
• Artemis II (April 2026): The Orion spacecraft carried a four-person crew around the Moon on a flyby test mission and captured the first crewed images of the lunar far side since the Apollo era, though it did not land. [1] (https://www.nasa.gov/artemis-ii-multimedia/), [2] (https://www.nasa.gov/gallery/lunar-flyby/)
• Artemis IV (Scheduled for 2028): NASA's first crewed lunar surface landing under the Artemis program will use Orion to dock in lunar orbit before astronauts transfer to a commercial human landing system to land on the Moon. [1] (https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-iv/), [2] (https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/artemis/)
