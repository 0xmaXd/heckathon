    Is it okay to land in the Moon's biggest crater in real life?Yes—in fact, NASA’s Artemis missions target the southern rim of the South Pole–Aitken (SPA) basin, which is the largest and oldest impact crater on the Moon (~2,500 km wide and up to 8–10 km deep). Because the impact blasted deep into the lunar mantle ~4.3 billion years ago, it exposed rare minerals right on the surface. In real life, spacecraft land on the high sunlit ridges along the basin’s rim (near Shackleton and Malapert) rather than deep on the dark floor—giving your base near-continuous sunlight for solar power and direct line-of-sight to Earth for communications, while keeping permanently shadowed craters just a short rover drive away for mining water-ice.
